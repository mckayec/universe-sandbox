James translations: M. R. James, *The Apocryphal New Testament* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1924), hosted by Early Christian Writings (Peter Kirby), except Apocalypse Paul at Wikisource. James is a historical translation/editorial synthesis, not a current critical edition. His reconstruction, variant parentheses and obsolete dating judgments must remain visible as editorial matter. Original Greek, Coptic, Latin and Syriac manuscripts were not independently collated. Acts Andrew includes all38 units of James's abstract of Gregory's epitome (**mediated synopsis evidence**), the actual Vatican Gr808 prison fragment1–18, detached quotations and a composite martyrdom. The full ancient original is lost; these witnesses do not restore it. Acts Peter changes base from Latin to Greek atXXX. Acts Thomas switches Greek witnesses and compares Syriac; prayer144–148 alternatively follows167.

Protevangelium uses the Roberts–Donaldson translation on the linked host. Gospel Peter's linked body translator was not securely identified on that page; no new permission is asserted. The host's Infancy Thomas Greek A page has a wrong James header: identification rests on its Thomas body and I–XIX sequence. The Thecla sequence misprints 33 as 38; CSV records both locator and correction without silently rewriting the witness.

Gospel Thomas: Stephen Patterson and Marvin Meyer, Scholars Version in *The Complete Gospels*, ©1992/1994 Polebridge Press. Copyright applies; only concise paraphrase and locators are included. Gospel Mary: the hosted English translation's attribution/reuse licence is not established here; no raw text is republished. Gospel Judas: Mark M. Mattison, *The Gospel of Judas*, gospels.net, expressly dedicated to the public domain by its translator; bracketed lacunae and conjectures remain distinct from surviving letters. Historical James/Roberts translations are old public-domain editions in the US; host design/modern annotations are not thereby licensed. No wholesale source captures are committed.

## Scholarly witness controls actually consulted

- Eric M. Vanden Eykel, [Protevangelium of James](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/protevangelium-of-james/), e-Clavis, updated September 2023, ECCA 611. Latter second-century composition; this is not a birth-event date.
- Tony Burke, [Infancy Gospel of Thomas](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/infancy-gospel-of-thomas/), updated May 2023, ECCA 421. Greek A is one recension; earlier versions lack several of its units. Do not import Greek D's Egypt stories into Greek A.
- Mark G. Bilby, [Gospel of Peter](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-peter/), 2025, ECCA 670. Fragment without original title/start/end; related papyri require separate identification.
- Christopher W. Skinner, [Gospel of Thomas](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-thomas/), 2019, ECCA 682. Coptic collection and Greek Oxyrhynchus witnesses are not interchangeable editions.
- Judith Hartenstein, [Gospel of Mary](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-mary/), 2018, ECCA 323. Berlin Codex pages 1–6 and 11–14 missing; Greek fragments overlap, not a recovered missing vision. No extant geographic setting.
- David Brakke, [Gospel of Judas](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-judas/), 2025, ECCA 860. Mid-second-century Greek composition; fourth-century Coptic Codex Tchacos pages 33–58, with losses. Composition, manuscript and narrated time are separate fields.
- Nathan Johnson, [Acts of Andrew](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/acts-of-andrew/), 2024, ECCA 788. Fragmentary original, multiple martyrdom witnesses and Gregory's epitome; Acts Andrew and Matthias is not automatically an original episode.

Other composition dates remain unresolved in this checkpoint. James's c.160 Acts Paul, pre-200 Acts Peter, c.260 Acts Andrew and late-fourth-century Apocalypse Paul estimates are **historical editor opinions**, not newly verified modern consensus. Narrative apostolic settings do not establish first-century authorship. No manuscript dates have been converted into narrated-event dates.
