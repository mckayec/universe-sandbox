# Independent sampled NT integration audit

Audited baseline: `0f527326dee62df7cd69ff1f7c446d9261b12867` (2026-10-26). Read the baseline NT integration CSV, research dossier and source register; independently reopened cited passages for **eight selected claims among 59 narrative clusters**. Selection emphasizes route attribution, person identity, omitted versions, and uncertain locations. This is not a rereading or certification of all 847 read-ledger units, nor a manuscript collation.

**Verdict: five sampled cases pass as written; one localized correction and two precision improvements are recommended.** No sampled case establishes historical geography, a historical miracle, or an execution date. Worker files were not edited; proposed replacements below are for root application to both the dossier and corresponding CSV summary.

## Findings

### 1. `nta-ape-peter-arrival` — correct the hosting clause

Opened Acts Peter IV–VI, particularly VI's reception and departure. The route and Theon's voyage baptism pass. Ariston is Theon's usual host and prepares to receive Peter, but Peter refuses to stay even one day. Calling Ariston Peter's host can create an unsupported lodging event.

Replace `Ariston hosts at Puteoli, Narcissus at Rome.` with:

> Ariston meets Peter at Puteoli; Peter declines to stay and proceeds to Narcissus's abode in Rome.

Source: [James, Acts Peter IV–VI](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actspeter.html).

### 2. `nta-ape-martyrdom` — pass, selected route/identity claims

Opened XXXII–XXXIII. Simon is carried Rome→Aricia→Terracina; Agrippa is designated prefect. Thus the route and refusal to identify him automatically as a Herodian king are supported. This check did not independently re-audit every later burial/end-variant detail in this cluster.

Source: [James, Acts Peter XXXII–XXXIII](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actspeter.html).

### 3. `nta-aj-inn` — preserve heading attribution

Opened the transition before 58 and 58–61. Ephesus is explicit in the body; Laodicea is supplied by a manuscript heading reported by James (host spelling `Laodicca`). The route is supported, but its source layer should remain visible.

Replace `Laodicea→Ephesus;` with:

> A manuscript heading reported by James gives Laodicea→Ephesus; the body explicitly narrates departure toward Ephesus.

Keep the existing inn and lacuna limits. Source: [James, Acts John heading before 58; 58–61](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actsjohn.html).

### 4. `nta-aj-christ-discourse` — pass, cave and visionary level

Opened 97–99. Mount Olives, the cave, Jerusalem below, and the distinction between the luminous cross and the wooden cross are explicit. The cluster correctly attributes the account rather than harmonizing it into ordinary gospel geography. No shrine coordinate follows.

Source: [James, Acts John 97–99](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actsjohn.html).

### 5. `nta-at-hymn` — pass, embedded route and omission

Opened 108–109, 111–113 and the note immediately after 113. The prince's route belongs to the recited hymn, not Thomas's physical itinerary. James explicitly omits the separate Syriac Song of Praise. Existing lexical/version warnings are appropriate; his claim about the hymn's earlier composition is editorial opinion, not independently dated here.

Source: [James, Acts Thomas 108–113 and following note](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actsthomas.html).

### 6. `nta-at-death-relics` — pass with distance precision

Opened 164–170. The unnamed mountain, four executioners, royal burial and later Mesopotamian transfer are supported. The mile in 164 ends where the king hands Thomas to soldiers, before the ascent; it is not a measured city-to-tomb distance.

Replace `One mile out of city, unnamed mountain, four soldiers spear Thomas, royal tomb.` with:

> After walking one mile outside the city, the king hands Thomas to soldiers; they take him up an unnamed mountain, where four soldiers spear him. He is buried in a royal tomb.

Source: [James, Acts Thomas 164–170](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actsthomas.html).

### 7. `nta-pt-scene` — pass, Thecla relationships and later endings

Opened II.27–43 and the following editorial appendix notice. Falconilla is Tryphaena's deceased daughter; Tryphaena faints and later receives Thecla. Myra→Iconium→Seleucia is supported. The mountain-concealment and underground-Rome continuations are explicitly additional manuscript endings, correctly excluded from the mandatory route. The host's first `38` between 32 and 34 is visibly misnumbered, supporting the ledger warning.

Source: [James, Acts Paul II.27–43 and following note](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actspaul.html).

### 8. `nta-gm-scene` — pass, unidentified soul and missing geography

Opened the complete short hosted extant body, especially 5:8–11, the missing-page notice, 8:10–24 and 9:1–10. The summary correctly leaves the ascending soul unidentified and asserts neither marriage nor a terrestrial setting. Hartenstein independently confirms the lost ascent beginning, absence of extant geographical location, and that the Greek fragments do not recover the missing text. Her Magdalene identification remains distinct from the body's name Mary.

Sources: [Gospel Mary, hosted extant text](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/gospelmary.html); [Judith Hartenstein, e-Clavis, summary and manuscript register](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-mary/).
