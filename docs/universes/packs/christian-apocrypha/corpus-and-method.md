## Defined atlas research inventory

1. **Deuterocanonical core:** Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch with Letter of Jeremiah,1/2Maccabees and Greek additions to Esther/Daniel. Work-level labels preserve the Catholic and other reception distinctions; chapter ledgers expose unreviewed units.
2. **Expanded OT collections and related pseudepigrapha:** English Apocrypha1/2Esdras, Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm151,3/4Maccabees; Enoch and Jubilees; explicit Meqabyan and broader Ethiopian source-access accounting.4Baruch and Ascension of Isaiah receive separate work entries rather than being folded into Baruch/Isaiah.
3. **NT-related narrative and sayings works:** James's infancy narrative, Infancy Thomas, Gospel Peter, Gospel Thomas, Mary and Judas; major apostolic Acts (Paul/Thecla, Peter, John, Andrew, Thomas); Peter/Paul apocalypses; the Nicodemus/Pilate and descent traditions. Each selected recension must be identified. A fragment's missing beginning/end is not “no atlas material.”
4. **Reception and wider boundaries:** Ethiopian broader church-order books, Apostolic Fathers, later infancy/Marian/Pilate cycles and other large collections are explicitly tracked as unintegrated rather than silently called completed. They are not all one genre. The current named inventory is a substantial research target, not a declaration that everything outside it is unimportant.

Before whole-pack readiness, material omissions must receive an explicit inclusion, source-blocked or justified scope disposition. Merely naming every tradition in a table does not complete its research. No scope or canon approval is inferred from the provisional inventory.

## Record and rendering contract

- Separate narrative time, attributed authorship, scholarly composition range, earliest manuscript witness and modern edition/publication date. An old character name is not an author's verified identity.
- Source body reading, summary-only access, fragment survival and source unavailability have distinct ledger statuses. Record URL, edition, section, actual read scope and retrieval date. Hash only a capture that actually exists; artifact hashes prove neither source capture nor reading.
- Places can be narrative, historically identified, proposed or unknown. Reuse prior Bible/Abrahamic geographic evidence by exact artifact/source reference; never copy an unsupported coordinate. Unknown sites and heavenly/underworld settings remain nonspatial reference entries. No default(0,0), no inferred miracle route and no altitude for a heaven.
- Use work-specific local reading gates during research, then assign stable global gates in an explicit mapping. These gates are navigation order, not a universal canon order or composition chronology. Distinct versions do not inherit each other's details.
- Retain contradictory testimony and hostile rhetoric as features of a particular text. Do not turn accusations into facts about a religious or ethnic population. Narrator, character and modern scholar are different claim speakers.
- Ancient works and older translations do not confer rights over modern translations, annotations, scans or websites. Paraphrase short findings, cite sections and link readers to lawful hosts. Do not publish wholesale source captures, manuscript images or copied modern translation text. Record each edition's rights context separately.
