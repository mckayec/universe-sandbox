## 2. Evidence structure

(C) means the listed passage was actually opened and read in its specified edition, not that the narrative happened historically. Preserve four layers: narrated action/speech; community canon/reception; editorial historical assessment; proposed geographical identification. Chapter record IDs are stable evidence bundles, not a claim that every person/place in a bundle is already an atomic engine entity. Listed journeys can be emitted as ordered nonspatial associations; promised, remembered, dreamed and supernatural journeys need distinct types. No edge called parent-of may be inferred from teacher, protector, royal brother, personified mother-city, or angel disguise.

USCCB notes explicitly identify historical/geographical tensions in Tobit and Judith. Judith’s Assyrian Nebuchadnezzar, postexilic temple and impossible campaign pace stay together as literary features, never a harmonized historical campaign. Bethulia remains unlocated. Esther additions have edition-specific numbering and royal names; Daniel additions have recension differences. See sources for exact limitations.

## 3. Place/reference disposition

All named material places are captured in the chapter payloads below and the CSV. Shared labels such as Jerusalem, Nineveh, Babylon, Ecbatana, Rages, Egypt and Judea may link to a parent Earth place card, but each work’s assertion stays source-scoped. Unknown sites (including Tobit’s Thisbe/Phogor/Kaserin and Judith’s Bethulia/Betomesthaim) remain separate unlocated references. Similar spellings are aliases only when the edition mapping supports it; no automatic merge of every Raguel/Raphael/Joakim/Ptolemy. Long regional summons lists in Judith are imperial narrative scope, not completed troop itineraries. Poetic Hades/heaven/Wisdom dwellings do not acquire terrestrial coordinates.
