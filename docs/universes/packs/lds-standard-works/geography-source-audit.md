## Exact proposed copy and evidence boundaries

**Heartland model note, supplementing the existing family description:** “Rod Meldrum argues for a North American setting by connecting Book of Mormon promised-land passages with latter-day revelations about the New Jerusalem in Missouri. This is his interpretation, not an official Church geography statement.” [GA1]

Meldrum gives prophetic and covenant language priority over reconstructed internal maps and interprets national liberty language through a United States setting. The reprint supports that rationale; it does not resolve the Niagara/Lake Michigan variants or authorize Mississippi/Sidon geometry. Its original publication/version date was not established here. Retain separate variant labels and the existing no-detailed-pins restriction.

**Nahom evidence note, replacing the assertion that no primary report was checked:** “An original epigraphic catalogue records three Barʾān altar inscriptions bearing a donor's NHM affiliation. Connecting that evidence with scriptural Nahom is an interpretive proposal; these inscriptions do not identify Ishmael's burial.” [GA2; final sentence distinguishes inscription contents from the proposed scriptural connection]

Nebes's p.119 nos.11–13 identify DAI Barʾān **1988-2**, **1994/5-2**, and **1996-1**, with the donor affiliation transliterated `nhmyn`, and classify them as early Sabaic period B (`aSab B`). Catalogue no.56's **1988-1** is a different prohibition text: preserve identifiers by publication rather than silently conflating them. The catalogue retains uncertainty over a dedication term's referent; do not turn every element into a certain translation. Text extraction around the chronological discussion has an apparent era anomaly; no exact annual date is adopted from it. The report supports ancient inscriptional evidence, not a located grave or a verified Lehite itinerary.

**Khor Kharfot model note:** “Warren Aston proposes Khor Kharfot as Bountiful and considers both Wadi Sayq and Wadi Kharfot possible approaches. His 2024 paper favors the latter's shorter, easier terrain without identifying either route as established.” [GA3]

The paper reports 2020–2024 examination and compares terrain, drainage and access. Its reported route lengths are author measurements, not atlas-verified geometry. Its inferred arrival season and suggested later shelter changes are model reasoning, not details supplied by 1 Nephi. Do not convert these into dated events. Modern road construction also limits straightforward comparison of present terrain with an ancient route. This is an independent faith-oriented publication, not a Church geography determination.

**Khor Rori model note:** “George Potter proposes Khor Rori as a harbor candidate, emphasizing maritime resources and shipbuilding requirements. The proposed link to Nephi remains uncertain.” [GA4]

Potter attributes the earlier proposal to his work with Richard Wellington and acknowledges uncertainty about the exact site. Note64 says relevant ship rock art has not been scientifically dated and reports an alternative dating later than the narrative's setting. It therefore cannot by itself establish shipbuilding facilities or expertise there at the required time. His conclusion concedes unconfirmed port use in Lehi’s time; ship dimensions and Ophir identification remain hypotheses. Keep Khor Rori and Khor Kharfot as distinct attributed proposals; neither the paper's engineering assumptions nor its proposed historical setting become a scriptural requirement.
