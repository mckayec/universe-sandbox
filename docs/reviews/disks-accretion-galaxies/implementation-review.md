## Findings

### DAG01 — High: saving during slow infall loses the capture state and can permanently hold stellar evolution

Evidence: [infall.ts:35–52](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/physics/infall.ts#L35) saves the previous `holdStage` inside `food.infall.heldStage` and sets `food.holdStage = true`. [history.ts:69–121](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/state/history.ts#L69) serializes `holdStage` but no `infall`. Neither [SavedBody](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/sandbox-state.ts#L1) nor `restoreBody` restores the capture record. `advanceStar` returns early for held stars (`stellar-evolution.ts:109`).

Trigger: save/undo/recovery while an ordinarily evolving star is partway through capture. Reload retains its diminished mass, current position/velocity and forced `holdStage=true`, but loses the eater ID, elapsed stripping time, starting mass and original held flag. The scripted continuation is lost; another capture can treat the forced true flag as the original state. This is a source-proven state-loss path, **not a browser-observed demonstration**. It is additional to the disclosed loss of galaxy tracer pictures. The scripted Slow Feast star already starts held, so testing only that preset would miss the evolution-hold regression.

**Fix:** persist and validate the infall state (including its original held flag), restore it after all body IDs exist, and define cancellation when the eater is missing/invalid. Alternatively, explicitly cancel capture at snapshot/restore while restoring the original held flag and documenting that semantic; do not silently retain an orphaned hold. Preserve old-save compatibility.

**Acceptance:** unit round-trip of a captured initially unheld star, an initially held star and a giant planet at several progress fractions; compare remaining mass/time and eater reference before/after. Remove the eater and verify cancellation restores the correct original flag. Check missing/invalid IDs, NaN/negative durations and legacy saves. Once browser access is permitted, independently exercise Save→Load, undo/redo and session recovery mid-capture; the current smoke test's body count alone does not establish this.

### DAG02 — Medium: slow-accretion copy presents a paced capture rule as a physical tidal-disruption prediction

Evidence: [exhibits.ts:59–65](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/exhibits.ts#L59) promises a star passing the tidal radius around 70 seconds and being stripped in half a minute. `infallDuration` is a clamped mass-based exhibit timer, then divided by `transferFactor`; capture is initiated from the collision-reach rule (`collisions.ts:253,302–306`), not a resolved relativistic disruption calculation. The donor is moved on a prescribed planar shrinking path and mass is transferred directly (`infall.ts:80–111`). These are acceptable display choices if identified as such. Pulsars also use this path and do not have black-hole horizons, despite generic horizon terminology in its comments.

[NASA's TDE visualization](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/20228/) supports disruption into streams and a disk, but also depicts expelled material; it does not establish universal total swallowing or the exhibit's durations. [NASA Anatomy](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/anatomy/) distinguishes emission outside the horizon from matter crossing it. Do not imply the bright stream is emitted from inside a black hole.

**Exact replacement objective:** “At 1× in Exhibit mode, watch a gas cloud feed the disk, followed by a star on a close approach. Capture and stripping are paced for this demonstration, over seconds to minutes rather than physical astronomical times. Select the black hole to follow its mass. This simplified model does not resolve stellar disruption, disk viscosity or outflows.”

**Exact event replacement:** “In this demonstration, {eater} captures {food} and transfers its mass through a simplified disk over about {duration} simulation seconds.” Use “surface” or neutral “compact object” language for pulsars rather than a horizon. Label the cloud-collapse exclusion as a proximity heuristic, not a universal rule that star formation cannot occur near compact objects.

**Acceptance:** copy makes pacing and omitted physics visible in each new exhibit/event; test timing expectations separately for Exhibit/Educational/Realistic modes, since `transferFactor` changes them. No need to implement full hydrodynamics to meet this educational correction.

### DAG03 — Medium: galaxy merger copy conflates an illustrative core-coalescence trigger with black-hole binary evolution

Evidence: [galaxy-merger.ts:42–49](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/bodies/galaxy-merger.ts#L42) announces merged central black holes; [exhibits.ts:113–119](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/exhibits.ts#L113) describes halo friction bringing them together until merger. Actual acceptance is contact plus a relative-speed cutoff of 8 (`galaxy-merger.ts:82–94`); there is no binary hardening or gravitational-wave evolution. [Porter et al. 2024, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04089) explicitly distinguishes a galaxy merger from the subsequent black-hole binary and later coalescence. No physical delay is calculated here.

**Exact replacement objective:** “Run at 5× or faster to explore a compressed galaxy encounter. Gravity redirects stellar orbits and can draw out tidal tails. A simplified drag rule brings these model cores together; their final replacement by one core is illustrative, not a calculation of black-hole binary evolution. Most stars miss one another, while real galactic gas can collide and shock.”

**Exact ‘Galaxies merge’ event:** “The model has combined the cores of {A} and {B}. Their stars continue in the combined gravitational field. The final black-hole binary stages and the long astronomical timescale are not simulated.”

**Exact ‘A new galactic core’ event:** “{eater} replaces the core of {galaxy} in this simplified model. Stars continue to respond to the combined core and halo field.” This avoids teaching that all stellar orbits depend on a single black hole alone.

[ESA's collision description](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2019/03/Colliding_galaxies) supports **rare physical stellar collisions**, not stars literally interpenetrating. [ESA's ISO observations](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/From_galaxy_collisions_to_star_birth_ISO_finds_the_missing_link) distinguish gas shocks. The code suppresses star-star contact for inter-galaxy pairs; call that a collisionless approximation, not an assertion that collisions are impossible. Acceptance is corrected wording and tests demonstrating the simplified fast-pass/slow-core branches without claiming a physical binary solution.

### DAG04 — Medium: blanket momentum-conservation claim exceeds the implemented halo/friction model

Evidence: [galaxy-dynamics.ts:4–8](https://github.com/mckayec/universe-sandbox/blob/fdc0147ff861f2f12c2773fdcb12d03cdbda70b6/src/physics/galaxy-dynamics.ts#L4) claims galaxy-by-galaxy momentum conservation. `galaxyMass` counts core plus halo, but excludes massive members. Halo gravity applies an enclosed-mass field to individual eligible bodies without accumulating reciprocal impulses on the halo anchor (`:68–90`). Friction coefficients are balanced using core+halo masses (`:94–108`), then applied to bodies with different radial weights and exclusions (`:110–123`). That algebra does not establish conservation of the full represented system. A locked anchor is also an explicit external constraint.

A simple algebraic counterexample for the friction claim: equal core+halo masses give opposite accelerations of equal magnitude. An extra massive member near one core receives nearly its core's acceleration; no corresponding member or recoil is added to the other side. Therefore the total massive-body impulse is nonzero. This is a source-level counterexample, not a measured drift from the rendered preset. The massless tracer model need not conserve an unmodelled self-consistent halo's momentum, but must not claim that it does.

**Fix:** remove the blanket conservation statement and describe the analytic halo plus phenomenological drag honestly, or implement a consistent aggregate mass/recoil scheme and verify it. Suggested comment: “Analytic halos and prescribed drag approximate encounter forces. They are not a self-consistent momentum-conserving simulation of stars and dark matter.” Preserve a similar model-limit statement in educational reference text if conservation is discussed there.

**Acceptance:** unequal halo scales/masses, unequal member populations, locked/unlocked cores and far tails have explicit conservation/error-budget expectations. Test the actual applied impulses, not merely the equal-and-opposite coefficient formula. Do not require exact global conservation while simultaneously retaining externally locked bodies.

## Physics and regression assessment

- **Disk motion:** `diskFlow` implements angular rate proportional to radius^-1.5; Newtonian circular balance gives omega=sqrt(GM/r^3) for a central point mass. The shared Kepler constant and spin remove rigid/counter-rotating layer behavior at source level. The phase crossfade refreshes texture, not physical gas parcels. View-space orbital tangents give the correct qualitative approaching-side sign; the Doppler polynomial and camera-facing lens arcs are illustrative, not relativistic ray tracing. NASA Anatomy supports the qualitative brightness asymmetry. Whether the shader looks stable from each camera angle remains unverified.
- **Trailing patterns:** the galaxy tangent decreases atan2(z,x), consistent with the chosen arm winding; tracer unit tests exercise orbit sense. This does not establish all rendered spiral morphologies, and no claim that real disks never precess should be added.
- **Encounter model:** massless tracers in softened core/analytic-halo fields can illustrate tidal deflection. The dynamic fields preserve separate pictures through merger in code. This is not a self-gravitating N-body/hydrodynamic model; stellar backreaction, gas shocks, full vertical dynamics and black-hole binary evolution are absent. The Toomre & Toomre original paper request timed out, so its exact methodological equivalence was not independently verified here.
- **Guided exhibits and Solar System tour:** the added test source checks retained planets/orbits and solar distance, and halo code deliberately excludes non-galaxy visiting systems. That is a pedagogical partition, not complete halo gravity for every object. The browser-based exhibit suite was **not run** in this review. Builder's 20-scene claim remains attributed evidence.
- **Persistence:** live tracer positions are intentionally not saved. `rebuildGalaxyVisuals` recreates one fresh backdrop per saved group; after a merger this also loses the original pair's tracer realization, not just animation time. Disclose that saving a merger does not preserve its tidal-tail picture. DAG01 concerns additional physical-state loss and needs fixing independently.
- **Mobile/performance:** source budgets are 90,000/22,000 base rendered stars, adjusted by style/quality, with 18/10 body members. The adaptive stride is capped at 6; its approximately 5 ms target is not a guaranteed frame budget. Every frame still writes the point buffers. Desktop/mobile memory, sustained frame time, pause/accelerated time and touch usability remain unverified; existing screenshots are not substitutes for interaction checks.
